SUBJECT: Submission of Manuscript P17 — Governance Philosophy and Architectural Ethics in Edge-Native Smart Environments

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Associate Editors,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled "Governance Philosophy and Architectural Ethics in Edge-Native Smart Environments" for publication consideration in AI & Society (Springer).

1. SUBSYSTEM BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
As AI sensing becomes ubiquitous in civic and educational spaces, ethics must transition from passive policy guidelines to hard architectural constraints. Relying on administrative promises or software settings to protect user privacy is fundamentally flawed. This paper formalizes a structural philosophy of architectural ethics for cyber-physical systems.

2. CORE TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTIONS AND NOVELTY
  - Canonical 8-Layer Stack Doctrine: Formalizes the unidirectional 8-layer stack architecture (L1 Substrate to L8 Federation), establishing hard structural separation boundaries between sensing and persistence.
  - Invariant Namespace (INV-01 to INV-15): Formulates 15 non-negotiable architectural invariants that prevent cross-layer data leakage, bypassable governance, or raw biometric exports.
  - Structural vs. Algorithmic Privacy: Proves that enforcing structural privacy-by-design at the architectural level eliminates the need for computational-heavy privacy trade-offs.

3. SUITABILITY FOR AI & SOCIETY
This paper fits AI & Society's scope in computational ethics, governance philosophy, structural privacy design, and the broader societal implications of autonomous edge sensing.

4. ETHICS AND ORIGINALITY DECLARATIONS
This manuscript is an original submission, is not under review elsewhere, and has been fully approved by all authors.
