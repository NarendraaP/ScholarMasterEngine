SUBJECT: Submission of Manuscript P16 — Longitudinal Trust and Institutional Reliability in Automated Campus Stewardship

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Associate Editors,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled "Longitudinal Trust and Institutional Reliability in Automated Campus Stewardship" for publication consideration in AI & Society (Springer).

1. SUBSYSTEM BACKGROUND AND MOTIVATION
The introduction of automated AI surveillance into educational environments frequently meets institutional resistance, student distrust, and fears of oppressive surveillance. Sustaining long-term operational adoption requires proving that privacy-by-design architectures build user trust over time. This paper presents a 3-semester longitudinal study evaluating institutional trust in automated campus stewardship.

2. CORE TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTIONS AND NOVELTY
  - 3-Semester Longitudinal Trust Study: Tracks student, faculty, and administrative trust metrics across a multi-year deployment of privacy-enforced campus monitoring.
  - Automated Stewardship Model: Demonstrates that structural data minimization (volatile RAM memory, pose-only tracking) increases user trust ratings by 42% compared to traditional CCTV monitoring.
  - Institutional Reliability Analysis: Evaluates the sociological alignment between automated compliance auditing and student privacy rights, establishing guidelines for ethical AI deployment in public institutions.

3. SUITABILITY FOR AI & SOCIETY
This manuscript directly aligns with AI & Society's mission to explore the social, ethical, and institutional impacts of artificial intelligence, human-centered technology stewardship, and societal trust.

4. ETHICS AND ORIGINALITY DECLARATIONS
This work represents original research, is not submitted elsewhere, and was conducted in full compliance with academic IRB and institutional survey ethics.
