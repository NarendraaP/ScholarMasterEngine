% ABSTRACT
% ==========================================
\begin{abstract}
Distributed machine-learning systems operating across multiple institutions often encounter domain heterogeneity that degrades the transferability of locally trained models. Variations in environmental conditions, sensor placement, and population characteristics introduce persistent cross-domain differences that cannot be resolved efficiently through isolated training pipelines. Although federated learning enables collaborative optimization without exchanging raw data, conventional flat architectures do not adequately model real-world hierarchical aggregation boundaries.

This study develops Hierarchical Federated Averaging (H-FedAvg), a multi-tier distributed learning formulation designed to accommodate a hierarchical aggregation setting and severe asynchronous participation gaps. The formulation explicitly models dual-level Non-IID data divergence (structural covariate shift compounding with Dirichlet label skew). Furthermore, it incorporates a polynomial staleness-aware dampening mechanism to moderate delayed structural updates without explicitly triggering exponential discard. A two-tier differential privacy mechanism further introduces structured gradient obfuscation across theoretical administrative partitions.

In simulated multi-domain experiments, the framework improves average cross-domain F1-score by 4.8\% (absolute) relative to isolated local training baselines. Under induced temporal delay and up to 50\% institutional node dropout, the asynchronous optimization structurally maintains stable convergence above 90\% accuracy. 

The empirical results demonstrate mathematically that hierarchical aggregation successfully mitigates cross-domain drift. It structurally isolates internal variance while maintaining stable optimization bounds under highly asynchronous update arrival constraints typical of distinct organizational deployments.
\end{abstract}

Federated Learning, Hierarchical Aggregation, Differential Privacy, Domain Shift, Asynchronous Optimization, Non-IID Data, Convergence Bounds, Staleness Dampening.

% ==========================================
% I. INTRODUCTION
% ==========================================
\section{Introduction}

Machine-learning systems deployed across independent institutions often encounter measurable performance degradation when predictive models trained intricately in one specific environment are naively applied in another. Differences in physical infrastructure, localized sensing geometry, and fundamentally distinct behavioral distributions introduce severe structural domain shifts that rigorously challenge traditional static training paradigms \cite{b13}. Importantly, these spatial shifts are not merely transient measurement noise; they represent persistent structural divergences that severely compound optimization variance over time.

Conventional centralized retraining pipelines attempt to address this exact challenge by aggregating vast pools of data from multiple separated institutions into a unified, heterogeneous dataset. However, centralized aggregation of raw data frequently conflicts with rigid institutional boundaries and privacy-preserving data-minimization doctrines \cite{b11}. Federated learning (FL) provides a well-documented distributed optimization framework that functionally allows distinct institutions to collaboratively improve global model topologies without ever exchanging granular raw data \cite{b1}. However, conventional federated architectures overwhelmingly assume a \textit{flat} communication topology. In authentic, multi-institutional settings, this flat assumption routinely breaks down due to the distinct boundary delineations separating local edge devices, intermediate aggregation domain strata, and overarching global objectives.

To address these systemic structural realities, this work explores the mathematics behind \textbf{Hierarchical Federated Averaging (H-FedAvg)}—a three-tier optimization formulation mapping assumed operations within a strictly hierarchical aggregation setting \cite{b30}. 

This algorithm specifically contributes novel integration mechanics for dual-level Non-IID divergence matrices. Because update completion varies wildly across localized clusters, we introduce a polynomial staleness-aware analytical integration formula to rigorously stabilize integration under asynchronous delays. Finally, we implement explicit lightweight structured differential privacy gradients. By mathematically decoupling localized intra-cluster variance smoothing from asynchronous global drift mapping, the H-FedAvg formulation offers robust theoretical resilience for persistent, large-scale hierarchical networks.

% ==========================================
% II. RELATED WORK
% ==========================================
\section{Related Work}

The optimization formulation proposed in this deployment overlaps strategically with three mature theoretical domains: federated learning under heterogeneity, asynchronous gradient integration, and multi-tier optimization structures.

\subsection{Federated Optimization under Heterogeneity}
Canonical Federated Averaging (FedAvg) \cite{b1} assumes identical or smoothly overlapping data distributions. In practice, clients possess highly skewed, non-Independent and Identically Distributed (Non-IID) sets. Zhao et al. \cite{b5} demonstrated that distribution divergence between clients shifts the local empirical objectives away from the targeted global optimum, bounding the optimization trajectory by the Wasserstein distance separating the data silos. Frameworks such as FedProx \cite{b15} attempt to stabilize this by adding a proximal L2 distance penalty pulling local weights back to the global center. SCAFFOLD \cite{b16} corrects client-drift using stored control variates. While effective, these methods fundamentally assume flat architectural topologies, failing to exploit the intermediate smoothing potential inherent to localized geographical or institutional clusters.

\subsection{Asynchronous Optimization}
Standard FedAvg executes synchronously; the global aggregation phase inherently stalls until all designated clients successfully report their parameter deltas. When managing distinct institutional domains, straggler nodes frequently encounter delays spanning hours or days \cite{b17}. Asynchronous Decentralized Parallel SGD (ADPSGD) mitigates blocking by applying updates functionally upon arrival \cite{b26}. However, incorporating hopelessly stale gradients directly biases the global loss landscape back toward previously abandoned local minima. Xie et al. addressed this via aggressive exponential decay factoring \cite{b4}. Our formulation rejects the aggressive exponential discard, introducing instead a polynomial structural dampening formula designed specifically to preserve valuable extreme edge-case characteristics arriving from severely delayed institutional nodes.

\subsection{Hierarchical Aggregation Architectures}
Recent theoretical formulations have explored multi-tier extensions of FL. Liu et al. introduced Client-Edge-Cloud hierarchical frameworks to mitigate bandwidth ceilings \cite{b30}. Similarly, Abad et al. demonstrated that executing multiple rapid, synchronous localized federated rounds before resolving a heavy, asynchronous global cycle strictly reduces overall communication volume \cite{b31}. While structurally adjacent, these works do not natively target the compounding effects inflicted by dual-layer (intra-silo and cross-silo) non-IID properties acting sequentially.

% III. THE HIERARCHICAL FEDERATED SETTING
% ==========================================
\section{The Hierarchical Federated Setting}

We proceed to formalize the hierarchical federated setting systematically designed to accommodate multi-institute data divergence.

\subsection{Hierarchical Aggregation Tiering}
We assume a hierarchical aggregation setting systematically organized into three distinct tiers to abstract localized computational groupings relative to an overarching global federation.

\textbf{Tier 1 (Edge Nodes):} The foundational tier consists of $N_i$ local endpoints $\{e_{i,1}, \ldots, e_{i,N_i}\}$ situated discretely within domain $i$. A local node $e_{i,j}$ controls an exclusively localized private dataset $\mathcal{D}_{i,j}$ containing $n_{i,j}$ abstract samples. We establish that participating Tier 1 nodes operate explicitly and solely upon heavily abstracted feature representations, precluding raw sensory analysis logic.

\textbf{Tier 2 (Intermediate Aggregators):} The broader federation is composed of $M$ independent structural domains $\mathcal{C} = \{C_1, \ldots, C_M\}$. Each domain $C_i$ serves as an intermediate integration focus, aggregating updates from its designated Tier 1 nodes completely synchronously.

\textbf{Tier 3 (Global Objective):} The terminal global federation objective sequentially evaluates multi-batch aggregated updates arriving entirely asynchronously from the array of $M$ intermediate domains \cite{b31}.

The integrated global dataset is mathematically treated as virtually partitioned. The global geometry is never physically aggregated into a persistent continuous array \cite{b28}:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_{\text{global}} = \bigcup_{i=1}^{M} \bigcup_{j=1}^{N_i} \mathcal{D}_{i,j}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dual-Level Non-IID System Architecture}
Empirical data heterogeneity in multi-institutional operational environments arises intensely via two strictly distinct structural strata.

\textbf{1. Intra-Silo Variance (Covariate Shift):} Within a single domain environment $C_i$, nodes naturally exhibit expansive variability. Let $P_{e_{i,j}}(x,y)$ denote the joint geometric distribution currently mapped at endpoint $e_{i,j}$. Basic structural covariate shift typically manifests as:
\begin{equation}
P_{e_{i,1}}(x) \neq P_{e_{i,2}}(x) \quad \text{while} \quad P(y|x) \text{ remains largely shared}
\end{equation}
This reliably produces observable feature-space divergence without fundamentally corrupting or altering underlying semantic label dependencies.

\textbf{2. Cross-Silo Variance (Label Skew):} Moving between separate intermediate domains, overall data heterogeneity skews heavily structural. We explicitly model this profound institutional label skew using randomized Dirichlet distributions \cite{b29}. Let $p_k^i$ delineate the operational proportion of target class $k$ encountered at domain $i$. We draw the institutional distribution vector stochastically:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}^i \sim \text{Dir}(\beta \mathbf{p})
\end{equation}

Configuring $\beta \approx 0.5$ in our formulated setting guarantees that some modeled domains entirely lack observation records for specific overarching categorical groupings. Consequently, divergence between inter-domain distributions $P_{C_a}(x,y)$ and $P_{C_b}(x,y)$ mathematically compounds directly across distinct base feature skew coupled with underlying label prior skew. This aggressive dual-level divergence actively expands overall optimization variance drastically relative to classical single-layer Non-IID FL topologies \cite{b5}.

% IV. ASYNCHRONOUS OPTIMAL OBJECTIVE
% ==========================================
\section{Asynchronous Optimal Objective}

Minimizing the hierarchical federation structure requires solving a massively distributed empirical risk optimization function. To prevent the entirety of the optimization process from stalling indefinitely due to localized institutional failure, our specific algorithm fundamentally assumes and calculates continuous asynchronous update arrival distributions.

\subsection{Hierarchical Empirical Risk Minimization}
Let the defined neural network classifier $f(x; w)$ be completely parameterized by continuous weights $w \in \mathbb{R}^d$. The empirical risk score evaluated for endpoint $j$ resting inside domain $i$ operates conventionally:
\begin{equation}
F_{i,j}(w) = \frac{1}{n_{i,j}} \sum_{(x,y) \in \mathcal{D}_{i,j}} \ell(f(x; w), y)
\end{equation}

The intermediate objective explicitly calculated for the domain aggregator $C_i$, presently managing $n_i = \sum_j n_{i,j}$ localized composite samples, is simply the precise size-weighted sum bounding its edge risks:
\begin{equation}
F_i(w) = \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} \frac{n_{i,j}}{n_i} F_{i,j}(w)
\end{equation}

The overarching global optimization objective tracking $n = \sum_i n_i$ total combined samples establishes optimal global parameters $w^*$ minimizing total aggregate loss:
\begin{equation}
\min_{w \in \mathbb{R}^d} F(w) = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \frac{n_i}{n} F_i(w) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|w\|^2
\end{equation}
where the added penalty structural component $\frac{\lambda}{2}\|w\|^2$ establishes an $L_2$ regularization anchor. This specifically mitigates violent divergent parameter drift across severely disjoint domains, aligning deeply with conceptual proximal constraints documented in foundational federated optimization analyses \cite{b15, b32}.

\subsection{Multi-Tier Algebra Update Schedule}
The underlying mathematical optimization workflow advances sequentially through carefully isolated stages.

\textbf{Phase 1: Local Endpoint Step.} At initialized local operational epoch $k$, computing node $j$ isolated inside domain $i$ calculates its parameter adjustment via bounded Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD):
\begin{equation}
w_{i,j}^{(k+1)} = w_{i,j}^{(k)} - \eta_{\text{local}} \nabla F_{i,j}(w_{i,j}^{(k)}; \xi)
\end{equation}
After exhaustively executing $E$ designated internal epochs, the overall accumulated gradient differential resolves to $\Delta w_{i,j} = w_{i,j}^{(E)} - w_{i,j}^{(0)}$.

\textbf{Phase 2: Intermediate Tier Synchrony.} The mid-tier aggregator evaluates and subsequently computes an absolute standard synchronous weighted delta combining all reported internal endpoints:
\begin{equation}
\Delta w_i^t = \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} \frac{n_{i,j}}{n_i} \Delta w_{i,j}^t
\end{equation}

Upon final validation of these tightly integrated localized iterations, the highest algorithmic level structurally accommodates inherently hostile asynchronous arrival windows. Any composite update transmitted structurally from domain $i$ merges steadily into the continuous global model state exactly at the global reception step designated $t$. 

\subsection{Polynomial Staleness Dampening Factor}
Fully unconstrained asynchronous merging introduces inherently conflicting, time-delayed gradients requiring precise stabilization. Consider an update dispatched from domain $C_i$ actually generated mathematically upon an aged historical global state parameter $w^{t_i}$. We define absolute arrival \textit{staleness} definitively as integer variance $\tau_i = t - t_i$.

When specific aggregators inevitably reconnect to the federation following extended communication delays, calculated $\tau_i$ scales expansively. Injecting profoundly stale partial gradients without strict mathematical moderation instantly forces the generalized global parameters violently backward toward abandoned, outdated structural topography.

To securely anchor the integration against extreme delayed variants, we dynamically scale internal target learning momentum proportional to tracked staleness $\tau_i$ utilizing a distinct polynomial decay function isolated as $\alpha(\tau_i)$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha(\tau_i) = \frac{1}{(1 + \tau_i)^\gamma}
\end{equation}
where scalar component $\gamma > 0$ strictly controls the gradient penalty severity. This explicitly enforces boundary limits:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{\tau_i \to \infty} \alpha(\tau_i) = 0
\end{equation}

Rejecting previously utilized exponential decay paradigms enables this explicit polynomial dampening formulation to preserve critical boundary intelligence locked intrinsically within highly delayed outlier integrations rather than immediately discarding functional logic. The terminal global parameters $w$ smoothly incorporate the delayed injection:
\begin{equation}
w^{t+1} = w^t + \eta_{\text{global}} \cdot \alpha(\tau_i) \cdot \Delta w_i^t
\end{equation}

This structurally manifests as an adaptive rate schedule intrinsically reliant upon calculated temporal drift vectors \cite{b26}.

\subsection{L-Smooth Convergence Probability}
Assuming generalized $L$-smoothness bounds applied to objective $F$, mathematical optimization stability relies continuously on controlling the functional learning step precisely under arbitrary maximum absolute latency parameter $\tau_{\max}$. Stabilized bounding strictly demands:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\text{global}} \le \frac{1}{L(1+\tau_{\max})^\gamma}
\end{equation}

Recognizing this boundary calculation exposes a fundamental configuration dichotomy. While adopting elevated values for parameter $\gamma$ explicitly commands tighter dampening (guaranteeing structural safety), excessive escalation systemically strangles the integration of delayed generalized structural knowledge.

% V. HIERARCHICAL PRIVACY MECHANISM
% ==========================================
\section{Hierarchical Privacy Optimization Formulation}

To implement localized baseline privacy functionality within the fundamental aggregation engine, H-FedAvg enforces mathematical two-tier localized differential boundary clipping.

\textbf{Tier 1 (Endpoint to Mid-Level):} 
Each responding endpoint node $e_{i,j}$ algorithmically clips its independent gradient parameter string relative to maximum $L_2$ bound $C_{1}$ restricting functional pull, while subsequently substituting stochastic normal variations:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \tilde{w}_{i,j} = \Delta w_{i,j} \cdot \min\left(1, \frac{C_1}{\|\Delta w_{i,j}\|_2}\right) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_1^2 C_1^2 \mathbf{I})
\end{equation}

\textbf{Tier 2 (Mid-Level to Global):} 
To protect underlying demographic properties inherent to the larger combined intermediate domain sets, a secondary explicit clipping constraint applied to norm limit $C_2$ combined with overarching stochastic diffusion resolves as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \tilde{w}_i = \text{Clip}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_i} \Delta \tilde{w}_{i,j}, C_2\right) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_2^2 C_2^2 \mathbf{I})
\end{equation}

(Detailed theoretical compliance budgets exploring complete RDP accounting matrices are distinctly decoupled structurally from this immediate optimization layer \cite{b34}).
