% ABSTRACT
% ==========================================
\begin{abstract}
A primary challenge in automated student engagement tracking is the structural ambiguity of facial expressions during high-focus tasks. Standard computer vision models frequently misclassify indicators of high cognitive load—such as furrowed brows or fixed gazes—as negative affect (e.g., boredom or frustration). While modern cost-sensitive learning frameworks can mathematically penalize these asymmetric errors during training, they often obscure the decision boundaries required for pedagogical auditability. This paper proposes an interpretable logic layer designed to resolve this contextual ambiguity deterministically. By integrating real-time facial expression analysis with institutional schedule metadata and instructional signal density, the system dynamically adjusts engagement inference parameters. Experimental validation on the Sim-Class-24 staged simulation dataset demonstrates that explicit causal priors and deterministic hysteresis control reduce the Simulation False Negative Rate under High Load (Sim-FNR-HL) to 6.0\% (95\% CI [4.1\%, 7.9\%]). This represents a significant improvement over both vision-only baselines (42.0\%) and cost-sensitive Transformer configurations (22.0\%). Furthermore, ablation studies confirm that semantic lexical analysis provides critical marginal gains over standard prosodic fusion. These results reflect algorithmic proof-of-concept validation on scripted scenarios, establishing that deterministic logic improves interpretability without sacrificing Type-II error optimization.
\end{abstract}

% I. INTRODUCTION
% ==========================================
\section{Introduction}
The objective assessment of student engagement in large-scale educational environments remains a persistent computational challenge. While traditional pedagogical strategies rely on an instructor's intuition to gauge the ``room temperature''—the collective attention and responsiveness of the class—automating this process via Computer Vision (CV) has been hindered by the inherent ambiguity of visual signals. This represents a discrepancy between low-level visual features (pixels representing facial muscle movements) and high-level cognitive states (learning or confusion). Recent psychological studies suggest that facial movements alone are insufficient for reliable emotion inference without situational context.

Preliminary discussions with faculty at engineering institutions highlighted a limitation in existing affective computing models: the generation of False Negative alerts during high-intensity instructional periods. Standard datasets, such as FER-2013 or CK+, train models to associate furrowed brows and narrowed eyes with negative emotions like sadness or anger. However, in an academic context—particularly during complex derivations or coding sessions—such expressions often indicate ``Germane Cognitive Load'' rather than disengagement.

This misinterpretation leads to a phenomenon we term the ``Valence Discrepancy'': the more students concentrate on difficult material, the more ``disengaged'' a standard unimodal AI system perceives them to be. This renders purely visual analytics potentially counter-productive for STEM subjects, where ``productive struggle'' is a necessary component of the learning process. In this work, ``engagement'' denotes a context-adjusted attentional proxy, not a direct measure of learning, motivation, or internal cognitive state.

\textbf{Error Asymmetry and Interpretability:} Educational engagement detection exhibits strong error asymmetry. Type-II errors (falsely labeling productive struggle as disengagement) are significantly more costly than Type-I errors, as they risk generating false alerts during critical learning moments. While cost-sensitive Transformers and weighted cross-entropy loss functions can mathematically penalize Type-II errors during model training, they resolve into opaque weights that lack pedagogical explainability. If a student's engagement score drops, an instructor must know \textit{why}. Rather than relying on black-box heuristics, this work explicitly targets domain-specific asymmetry through deterministic causal priors. By anchoring a temporal hysteresis control loop to schedule metadata and instructional lexical density, the system maintains a fully auditable decision boundary. This approach reframes engagement inference as a pedagogical decision problem rather than a perceptual classification task.

\subsection{Relation to the ScholarMaster Research Series}
This paper forms part of the \textbf{ScholarMaster Research Series}, a broader initiative investigating intelligent systems for academic environments. While related studies examine biometric indexing, hardware efficiency, and deployment systems engineering, this paper focuses exclusively on the \textbf{Context Fusion Logic Module}. This module interfaces with perception systems via standardized context vectors; no biometric or identity data is accessed or persisted within this logic layer. This paper evaluates the context-fusion algorithms independently, assuming access only to anonymized valence vectors and schedule metadata.

% II. RELATED WORK
% ==========================================
\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Limitations of Unimodal Affective Computing}
Early automated monitoring systems relied predominantly on Facial Expression Recognition (FER). While effective for basic emotion detection in commercial settings, Whitehill et al. \cite{b7} argued that ``engagement'' is a distinct construct from ``emotion.'' Unimodal FER systems lack the requisite context to differentiate between ``frustration'' (a negative state blocking learning) and ``confusion'' (often a precursor to understanding). This leads to noisy analytics where productive struggle is flagged as a behavioral issue. Advanced deformable models and specific facial indicators of tutorial frustration have improved granularity but still struggle with context.

\subsection{Multi-Modal Learning Analytics}
Advanced frameworks have attempted to bridge this gap using physiological sensors (EEG, Galvanic Skin Response). However, such methods are invasive and non-scalable for typical university classrooms. Poria et al. \cite{b10} reviewed the necessity of multimodal fusion, and Atrey et al. surveyed methods for combining these distinct signals effectively.

Recent AudioVisual Transformer architectures achieve strong performance on generic sentiment analysis by learning late fusion weights. However, these black-box models optimize aggregate accuracy without accounting for domain-specific error asymmetry. In educational contexts, falsely labeling productive struggle as disengagement (Type-II error) is far more costly than missing true disengagement (Type-I error). Our deterministic logic layer explicitly encodes this asymmetry via pedagogical priors.

Acoustic analysis has typically been limited to prosodic features (pitch, tone, volume, MFCCs) to detect ``noise levels'' or emotional arousal. In our initial experiments, we implemented a multimodal baseline fusing visual expression vectors with prosodic features. While this approach improved FNR-HL from 42\% (vision-only) to 28\% (prosodic fusion), it still failed to differentiate between "frustrated questioning" and "deep concentration," both of which exhibit low pitch variance and neutral facial expressions during silent derivation phases. This motivated our shift to Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) to extract \textit{instructional signal density}—knowing what is being said establishes a reliable ground-truth prior.

\subsection{Cognitive Load Theory}
Sweller's Cognitive Load Theory posits that effective learning involves high ``Germane Load.'' Physically, this manifests in reduced microsaccades, increased facial tension, and a cessation of ``social'' expressions. By integrating schedule metadata, our system aligns its inference logic with these theoretical expectations. If the schedule indicates a ``Lab'' or ``Tutorial,'' the system expects high cognitive load and suppresses the penalty for neutral/negative facial valence.

\subsection{The 3-Stream Pipeline}
The system employs an asynchronous multi-modal pipeline to handle disparate data rates.

\subsubsection{Stream A: Visual Inference}
The visual stream isolates facial landmarks and generates abstract expression vectors using a standard ResNet-based backbone. The expression vectors are then processed by a lightweight valence estimation classifier to derive $V_{neg}$ (probability of negative affect). This stream provides high-frequency but semantically ambiguous data.

\subsubsection{Stream B: Acoustic Semantics (Measurement Oracle)}
Audio is continuously buffered. To provide a rigorous upper-bound validation of the semantic density hypothesis, the buffer is processed by the Whisper ASR model. The ASR component functions strictly as a transient semantic density estimator and does not expose, store, or reconstruct linguistic content at any resolution usable for interpretation or replay.

\textbf{Lightweight Semantic Density Model (Keyword-Based):}
To validate algorithmic viability without relying on heavy ASR dependencies, we also evaluate a lightweight keyword-spotting alternative:
\begin{equation}
C_{load}^{lite} = \frac{\sum_{w \in transcript} \mathbf{1}[w \in \mathcal{D}_{STEM}]}{|transcript|}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}_{STEM} = \{$``derivative'', ``integral'', ``matrix'', ``algorithm'', ...$\}$ is a domain-specific dictionary. Ablation studies (Section VIII) demonstrate that this lightweight model retains the majority of the Type-II error reduction benefit.

\subsubsection{Stream C: Temporal Context}
The system queries the institution's schema to retrieve the \texttt{Subject\_ID} and \texttt{Lecture\_Type} (e.g., "Lab", "Theory"). This acts as the prior probability distribution for the expected engagement behavior.

\subsection{Cross-Modal Synchronization}
A significant algorithmic challenge in multi-modal fusion is the temporal misalignment between high-frequency video frames and low-frequency semantic audio evaluations. We implement a \textbf{Timestamped State Latch}. The audio subsystem updates a shared \texttt{Current\_Context\_State} variable upon completion of an inference cycle. The video logic reads this variable non-destructively for every frame. While this introduces a brief ``Context Lag'', this delay is pedagogically acceptable as cognitive load states typically persist for several minutes.

% ==========================================
% IV. METHODOLOGY
% ==========================================
\section{Methodology}

The core contribution is the mathematical formalization of ``Contextual Re-weighting.'' We model the engagement score $E(t)$ not as a direct function of facial expression, but as a modulated function of context. The deterministic fusion logic operates independently of the formal spatiotemporal invariants defined in Paper 21.

\subsection{Derivation of the Re-weighting Equation}
Let $V_{neg}$ represent the visual probability of ``Negative'' affect (e.g., frowning). We introduce a Context Weight, $\beta$, determined by the schedule metadata (e.g., $\beta_{STEM} = 0.7$, $\beta_{ARTS} = 0.1$). 

The Audio Semantic Density, $C_{load}$ is calculated by counting the frequency of domain-specific tokens ($T_{domain}$) in the rolling window relative to total tokens ($T_{total}$):
\begin{equation}
C_{load} = \frac{\sum T_{domain}}{T_{total}}
\end{equation}

The final Engagement Score uses a sigmoid activation to softly switch modes based on $C_{load}$. The sigmoid curve provides a differentiable and explainable transition between "low load" (standard monitoring) and "high load" (tolerance mode).

\begin{equation}
E(t) = \alpha \cdot (1 - V_{neg}) + \beta \cdot \frac{1}{1 + e^{-k(C_{load} - \mu)}}
\end{equation}
Here, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are normalized such that $\alpha + \beta = 1$.
Algorithm 1 presents a discretized operational form of the continuous formulation in Eq. (3).

In Eq. (3), $\frac{1}{1 + e^{-k(C_{load} - \mu)}}$ represents the probability that the current session is ``High Load.'' The sigmoid term acts as a continuous weighting function.
\begin{itemize}
    \item If $C_{load} \ll \mu$ (Low Load), the term approaches 0, and $E(t) \approx \alpha(1 - V_{neg})$. Standard logic applies: frowns reduce the score.
    \item If $C_{load} \gg \mu$ (High Load), the term approaches 1, and the score is boosted by $\beta$. In this state, the penalty for negative valence is effectively neutralized or inverted.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Temporal Hysteresis}
To prevent the engagement score from flickering rapidly due to momentary pauses in speech or micro-expressions, we implement a hysteresis filter. The smoothed score $E_{smooth}(t)$ is defined recursively:

\begin{equation}
E_{smooth}(t) = \gamma E(t) + (1-\gamma) E_{smooth}(t-1)
\end{equation}

where $\gamma = 0.2$ is the smoothing factor. This ensures that a single frame of ``confusion'' does not immediately drop the engagement metric.

\subsection{Visual Pre-processing Pipeline}
Given the variable lighting conditions inherent in lecture halls (e.g., projector glare, dimmed lights), raw RGB frames are insufficient. We implement a pre-processing stage using \textbf{Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE)}. Unlike standard global equalization, CLAHE operates on small regions (tiles) of the image, enhancing local contrast while limiting noise amplification. This step is crucial for maintaining reliable face detection performance under low-light conditions, ensuring that the visual input vector $V_{vec}$ remains reliable even when the environment is suboptimal.

\section{Algorithm Design}

\subsection{Voice Activity Detection (VAD)}
Executing semantic extraction on continuous silence introduces unnecessary algorithmic overhead. We implement a lightweight Voice Activity Detector (VAD) prior to the semantic stage. Audio frames with an energy level below -40dB are discarded immediately. The semantic extraction is only triggered when the VAD probability exceeds 0.85 for a sustained duration.

\STATE
\STATE // Step 3: Visual Inference
\STATE $V_{vec} \leftarrow \text{Classify\_Valence}(V)$
\STATE
\STATE // Step 4: Decision Logic
\STATE // Discretized operational approximation of Eq. (3)

    \STATE // Apply context-aware valence reweighting

\section{Dataset Generation: Sim-Class-24}

To validate the model algorithms without compromising student privacy during the prototyping phase, we curated the \textit{Sim-Class-24} dataset. This controlled environment allowed us to isolate variables that are impossible to control in the wild.

\subsection{Simulation Protocol}
We constructed a ``Staged Simulation Environment'' to generate 24 distinct engagement sequences. Rather than relying on unpredictable real-world events, these sequences were deterministically scripted to model three specific 60-minute scenarios:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Scenario A (Baseline):} A non-technical discussion simulation where the visual input maintains neutral-to-positive expressions.
    \item \textbf{Scenario B (High Load):} A ``Deep Work'' simulation where the visual feed mimics the physiological markers of solving complex calculus (furrowed brows, fixed gaze) while the audio feed contains high-density STEM terminology.
    \item \textbf{Scenario C (Mixed):} A realistic timeline interlacing active listening with intermittent note-taking (simulated head-down occlusion).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Environmental Variability}
To prevent ideal-condition bias, we synthetically introduced environmental noise into the visual frames:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Occlusion:} We simulated 90-degree head turns and ``hand-on-face'' postures typical of bored or tired students.
    \item \textbf{Lighting Noise:} The video feed was subjected to variable gamma correction to mimic projector glare and dimmed lecture hall conditions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dataset Limitations}
We acknowledge that \textit{Sim-Class-24} is a synthetic dataset used for error isolation and does not fully capture the stochastic nature of real-world behavior. The scripted nature of the sequences ensures deterministic ground truth for algorithmic validation but lacks the nuance of spontaneous social interactions. Therefore, the results presented in this work should be interpreted as an algorithmic proof-of-concept for the fusion logic.

\subsection{Statistical Significance and Deep Baseline Comparison}
To rigorously evaluate the proposed logic layer, we introduced two advanced deep learning baselines: a Vision model utilizing Weighted Cross-Entropy (WCE-ResNet) to heavily penalize False Negatives, and a multimodal Cost-Sensitive Transformer (CS-Transformer) trained to implicitly fuse audio-visual features under asymmetric loss. 

While the CS-Transformer improved the Sim-FNR-HL to 22.0\%, it fell short of the deterministic logic layer (6.0\%). To confirm this reduction is not an artifact of the simulation's sample variance, we calculated the 95\% Confidence Interval for the proposed model's Sim-FNR-HL, bounding it at $[4.1\%, 7.9\%]$. Furthermore, a McNemar's test comparing the predictions of the CS-Transformer against the Proposed Context Logic yielded a highly significant difference ($\chi^2 = 14.2, p < 0.001$). 

Finally, to ensure the deterministic logic does not overfit to STEM scenarios, cross-scenario robustness was validated on Scenario A (Humanities/Discussion). The dynamic sigmoid activation successfully suppressed the $\beta$ weighting due to the absence of domain-specific lexical density, allowing baseline visual inference to proceed without artificially inflating the engagement score. This confirms the adaptability of the hysteresis control across varying pedagogical environments. The modest increase in FPR-LL (from 12\% to 13\%) reflects a deliberate algorithmic trade-off favoring Type-II error reduction, consistent with the asymmetric cost structure of educational engagement inference. To account for the limited participant pool ($N=8$) in the simulation, we measured per-subject variance, observing a standard deviation of $\pm 2.1\%$ in FNR across individuals, confirming the heuristic's algorithmic stability within the simulated cohort.

\section{Scope and Limitations}

\subsection{Evaluation Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simulated Ground Truth:} All quantitative results (Table I, Figure 2) are derived from the Sim-Class-24 staged simulation dataset. These scenarios were scripted to isolate the fusion logic from confounding real-world variables. **The reported Sim-FNR-HL improvements should not be interpreted as expected performance in uncontrolled environments** without in-situ validation.
    \item \textbf{ASR Module Status:} The semantic density analysis is implemented as a **measurement oracle** to validate the hypothesis that instructional lexical cues improve engagement inference. 
    \item \textbf{Population Generalization:} The Sim-Class-24 dataset was created using a limited participant pool. Expression patterns, cognitive load manifestations, and acoustic characteristics may not generalize across diverse demographics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ethical Considerations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The algorithm evaluates **aggregate classroom-level** metrics, not individual student tracking.
    \item No biometric identification is performed by the engagement module (identity-agnostic).
    \item Future implementations must comply with FERPA (US), GDPR (EU), and local privacy regulations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Architectural Irreversibility and Semantic Non-Persistence}
The semantic analysis components evaluated in this paper operate strictly as ephemeral, non-persistent measurement oracles. Instructional lexical cues are processed transiently and reduced to low-dimensional scalar indicators (e.g., semantic density scores) before immediate algorithmic destruction. No raw transcripts, token sequences, or reconstructed speech content are retained, logged, or made accessible to downstream logic.

Critically, the context-fusion logic does not possess the capability to reconstruct instructional speech or infer speaker identity. All semantic signals are constrained to one-way transformations that influence engagement weighting. This ensures that the system logic cannot be repurposed for content surveillance.

\subsection{Ephemeral Data Handling}
The algorithmic pipeline is designed assuming a ``Privacy by Default'' architecture where audio is processed entirely in volatile memory and immediately deallocated. Engagement scores are non-evaluative signals intended solely for aggregate situational awareness and are not designed to inform assessment, grading, or individual-level judgment. The proposed framework is intended as an algorithmic decision-support signal rather than an automated intervention trigger.

% X. DISCUSSION
% ==========================================
\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Why Prosodic Fusion Fails in Educational Contexts}
Our multimodal prosodic baseline (Sim-FNR-HL = 28\%) demonstrates that audio integration provides significant improvement over vision-only (42\%), validating the necessity of multimodal fusion. However, it still fails to prevent productive struggle misclassification. The root cause is semantic ambiguity:

**Case Study:** A student asking "I don't understand how to take the derivative of this function" exhibits:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Visual:} Furrowed brow (negative valence)
    \item \textbf{Prosodic:} Low pitch variance (hesitant speech)
    \item \textbf{Prosodic Fusion Interpretation:} "Frustrated/Disengaged" (Type-II error)
\end{itemize}

However, **semantic lexical analysis** reveals the keyword "derivative" → STEM context → productive struggle, not disengagement. This explains why our context logic (Sim-FNR-HL = 6\%) achieves 4.7x improvement over prosodic fusion (28\%).

\subsection{Why End-to-End AudioVisual Transformers are Insufficient}
Recent trends in multimodal learning favor end-to-end deep fusion networks. While effective for general emotion recognition, these models fail in educational contexts for three reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lack of Pedagogical Priors:} End-to-end models learn correlations, not causality. They cannot inherently know that "Calculus" implies "Expected Cognitive Load." Our deterministic logic layer explicitly encodes this domain knowledge via schedule metadata.
    \item \textbf{Error Asymmetry Blindness:} Transformers optimize aggregate accuracy, treating Type-I and Type-II errors equally. Educational contexts exhibit strong asymmetry.
    \item \textbf{Sparse Symbolic Context:} Educational metadata (e.g., schedule changes, subject IDs) are sparse, symbolic signals unsuitable for dense vector fusion. Our logic engine uses discrete lookups rather than learned embeddings.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion \& Future Scope}
This work introduces a context-aware logic layer that addresses the critical limitation of false negatives in unimodal student engagement analysis. By fusing visual data with semantic acoustic priors and schedule metadata, we reduced the **Simulation False Negative Rate under High Load (Sim-FNR-HL) from 42.0\% (vision-only baseline) to 28.0\% (multimodal prosodic fusion) to 6.0\% (proposed context logic)**, achieving a 6-fold improvement over vision-only baselines under controlled simulation conditions. Furthermore, we validate a lightweight keyword-based alternative that retains 80\% of the benefit, demonstrating algorithmic viability. The lightweight keyword model represents the intended production configuration; the Whisper ASR module serves as an experimental upper-bound oracle. These results support the hypothesis that semantic lexical analysis (not just prosodic features) is critical for Type-II error minimization in pedagogical engagement inference.

Future algorithmic iterations will explore the integration of micro-expression analysis to detect transient frustration states. Future work will seek to validate these logical models against in-situ data.
